# Changelog

What changed in Alnasl, newest first. The format loosely follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and each entry is
tagged so that a version can be skimmed:

| tag | meaning |
|---|---|
| [Feature] | something new you can do |
| [Fix] | something that was broken |
| [Change] | existing behaviour works differently |
| [Removed] | something is gone |
| [Security] | a hole closed, or judged not to be one |
| [Verified] | checked against the real thing, no code change |

A version is released when its number is in `mix.exs` **and** it has a
section here (`## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-03`): the release's page on GitHub is that
section, word for word. See [docs/releasing.md](docs/releasing.md).

## [Unreleased]

- [Change] The docs and the screens now call them what they are to the person using them: live boards.

## [0.2.1] - 2026-10-17

- [Fix] **The account menu is drawn over the page, not under it.** The rail is sticky, which makes it a stacking context, and without a level of its own it was painted in document order: the manual's contents, sticky and later in the page, landed on top of the theme buttons. The rail now has a level above the page, so what opens from it, the account menu and the hall picker, stays above everything on every page and theme.
- [Change] **An empty Boards page on the server talks about bridges.** It used to invite you to plug an electronic board into a serial port the server does not have. It now says boards arrive through bridges, lists this hall's bridges with their state and how many boards each reports, and links to the hall's Bridges tab; a hall with none is told so in one sentence. The box keeps its serial ports.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-17

- [Security] **Sharing a tournament is not sharing the keys to the building.** The best role in any hall a tournament is played in still counts for its share in that hall, and for nothing else: giving the current hall's tables away, adding a code or touching its bridges needs a role in the hall you are standing in; removing the tournament, its Lichess key, starting it by itself and the next round need one in its home hall; a result or a new game for board N needs one where board N is played. An arbiter of the annex who is a viewer in the main hall could seat the annex's tournament on the main hall's tables with one hand-made event; the event now gets the same answer the button would. *Also play it in this hall* works for the arbiter it is shown to, who until now was offered a button and refused by it - the hall's own code reaching the tournament is the permission. *Add a hall* starts after the tables where another tournament's boards sit, not only after the ranges it bothered to write down, and refuses in a sentence to take a table whose board has a game under way. A merge that moves a hall to later boards renumbers that hall's boards at once, a hall whose own tournament is played next door cannot be deleted from under it, rolling the placements migration back keeps the ranges of tournaments from before halls, a server no longer asks GitHub for updates it cannot install, and Live asks the database a handful of questions a refresh instead of a few per board.
- [Change] **A frame that knows which hall it is in, and a language that belongs to whoever reads it.** The rail is grouped - Play (Live, Boards, Games), Send (Results), Manage (Halls, Setup), Look back (Log, Recordings), Help - and its top names the box, or says *server* on the server. With more than one hall, a hall picker shows the hall on show and how many of its boards are live, lists your halls with your role and their live boards (a search field from seven on), and switching keeps you on the page you were on instead of dumping you on the front page to start over. The account button holds who you are, your role here, Account, the language, the colour theme and Sign out; the theme picker in Setup and the EN/NL at the foot of the rail are gone, because three ways to do one thing is two ways to wonder which one won. The language is per person now: kept with the account when there is one, in a browser cookie when there is not, set by a form instead of a link a crawler could follow. Whoever chose nothing reads the box's default, which an admin still sets in Setup; one arbiter switching to Dutch no longer switches the whole hall with them. On a phone the rail becomes a top bar and a tab bar - Live, Boards, Results, More - and nothing is out of reach. Notices stack, two at a time, the rest folded behind *N more notices*, and a notice about tournaments names three and sends you to Setup for the rest. Live shows the tournaments placed in this hall and, of those, the boards here and the games played here, so a tournament split over two rooms no longer shows each room the other's half. It offers a list next to the tiles - table, players, last move, clocks, state - remembered per browser and chosen for you from 24 boards on; the QR code waits behind *Share* instead of being drawn for every tournament on every refresh; and a move redraws its own tile, not the hall.
- [Change] **Two editions, one answer, and a front page that stops listing the universe.** `Alnasl.edition/0` says `:box` or `:server`, Setup's *This copy of Alnasl* says it too, and the server stops offering what needs a serial port or an installer: *Add a board*, the serial ports, simulators (on Boards and on PGN), Updates and starting with Windows, the PIN and the hall network, and the box's name, which is a fixed name there and turns the panel into *This server*. Absent, not greyed, and the events refuse when a clever client sends them anyway. The Live page draws a tile only for a board that exists here or has a game; pairings with neither become one line ("26 pairings without a board here"), and a tournament with neither gets no section and no tab, only a line saying how many there are. A box with one tournament still draws a tile for every pairing, and a tournament opened by its own address is always shown, counted like the rest. Ten tournaments of forty pairings used to be four hundred tiles for four boards.
- [Change] **Setup stops being a junk drawer.** Twelve panels on one page are seven sections, each with its own address and a list beside it (a drop-down on a phone): `/setup/tournaments`, `connections`, `results`, `this-box`, `access`, `updates`, `advanced`. A section the edition or the role cannot use is not drawn: the server has no Access and no Updates, and only an admin sees Updates and Advanced. Tournaments are a line each - name, a dot for running, round, boards, a tables chip - and open for the details, the halls and tables, and the automatic start; the ones with boards on this box come first, the rest are one folded line, the finished and the not yet started sit behind a toggle, and from the ninth tournament there is a search field. Connections are a line each with a state and a menu (Test, Retry, Remove), and *+ Add* holds the two ways in: a pasted code, or an OpenPairings account. Gone from Setup: the colour theme (it lives in the rail), and the bridges, who were only ever the hall page's guests; a button to the hall page is left in their place. Everything else is where it was, one click further, which is the usual price of tidying.
- [Change] **Every page asks which hall it is in.** Boards, the numbering and finding pages (which used to read every board on the box and number whichever one felt a piece lift), PGN, the Outbox with its counts and the number in the frame, the Log and the recordings now answer for the hall you are looking at, and the Log and the recordings take a tick, *All my halls*, for the rest. They also have an end: fifty lines or files a page and a search box, instead of the newest 300 that were filtered for your halls afterwards. A board's tournament select lists what is played in the hall and the tournament the board is in. *Halls* is a table instead of cards, and a hall has tabs - People, Invitations, Bridges, Settings - each with an address of its own. A box without accounts sees everything, as before.
- [Security] **The Games page asks where the board is, too.** Attaching a game to board N, or playing it on a simulator as board N, now needs your role in the hall board N is played in, the rule the Live page learned a while ago; an arbiter of the annex who watches the main hall no longer writes over the main hall's board 3 from a pasted PGN. One game on a simulator also plays for the tournament on show, as *Play them all* always did, instead of wandering off to the box's own. The count beside Results in the rail is the badge's count, from one place, so the two can no longer argue about what is waiting; and the links into Setup land on a section that exists, the bridges button on the hall's Bridges tab.
- [Feature] **A hall can be deleted.** On its page, an owner or an admin types the hall's name, the button wakes up, and the people, invitations, join codes and boards of the hall go in one transaction. A hall with bridges, tournaments or an OpenPairings connection left refuses and says which ("2 bridges and 1 tournament"), so the button cannot throw a game away, and the box's own hall cannot be deleted at all. Whoever was standing in it is moved to another hall of theirs, and open pages notice. Nothing is lost that was not asked for twice.
- [Feature] **One tournament in several halls, several tournaments in one hall.** A tournament no longer lives in one hall on one run of tables; Setup, under each tournament, *Halls and tables* lists where it is played: a hall, its tables, the first board they play. Boards 1-8 in the main hall and 9-16 in the annex are two rows of one tournament, and table 4 of the annex plays board 12. *Add a hall* offers only halls where you arbitrate or own, and starts at the next free tables and the board after the last one placed. Two tournaments on one table, one board in two halls, and a tournament with no hall left are refused; the Open on 1-8 and the under-twelves on 9-16 work exactly as before, because that was never the hard part. A tournament shows up in every hall it is played in, your role for it is the best one you have in any of them, and a board is still recognised only in its own hall - sharing a tournament is not sharing a room. A second hall's code for a tournament the box already has offers *Also play it in this hall* instead of merely pointing out that it exists elsewhere. Existing boxes are upgraded to one row per tournament, seated exactly as they were.
- [Feature] **A manual, with chapters, in English and Dutch.** Help is no longer one long page that was a guide before it was a manual. It is seven chapters - getting started, boards and ports, bridges, following games, the links to OpenPairings and OpenResults, accounts and halls, recording and troubleshooting - in the look of OpenPairings' manual, with a contents page, a search (press `/`), an outline per chapter, all in whichever of the two languages the box speaks. Beside the title of every other page sits a small *?* that opens the manual at the section about it, so nobody has to remember which chapter the Outbox lives in. Written from what the code does, which turned out to be a more modest feature list than the ambition.
- [Feature] **Connect a whole OpenPairings account instead of copying codes one by one.** Setup, *Connections*, *+ Add*, *Connect an OpenPairings account*: type the address once (or set `ALNASL_OPENPAIRINGS_URL`; the last one used is offered after that), sign in over there, press Allow, and you are back with every current tournament that account may edit - including the ones it has not made yet. The list shows it as "All your tournaments on host (N)". The answer that comes back is good once, for two minutes, and only for the browser that asked; a stale, stolen or cancelled one adds nothing and says so in a sentence. Owners and arbiters of the hall only, checked when you leave and again when you return, because people do change their minds in between.
- [Change] **The board drivers moved out, to Arkab.** The board protocol, the single board, the loop, the emulator, the transports, the capture format and the replay player now live in Arkab, a library of their own, so the code that reads a board exists once - here, and on a bridge. A board on the box's own port reads byte for byte as it did; only the module names changed (`Alnasl.Dgt.*` is `Arkab.Dgt.*`, and so on). The capture files are the same files.
- [Feature] **Bridges: boards from somewhere else.** A hall's page (or Setup, for the hall you are looking at), *Bridges*, *Add a bridge* shows a link and its QR code, good for fifteen minutes and one bridge. Paste or scan it on an Arkab bridge - a Raspberry Pi beside the boards of another hall - and that is the whole setup: the bridge fetches its own key, dials out over one WebSocket, and its boards turn up as rows of their own, numbered and linked like any other. A board carried from the box's own port to a bridge is recognised by its serial and keeps its table and its game. One box takes any number of bridges, and a bridge may belong to more than one box.
- [Feature] **Commands down the same line.** From the Bridges panel: read a port, read it again, forget it, and send every board again in full. Answers come back in words, including "no".
- [Feature] **Remove, from either side, ends it for both.** Removed here, a connected bridge is told to forget this box, and one that dials in later is told the same thing. Removed on the bridge, it is gone here too, with its boards. Nobody is left politely knocking on a door that has been bricked up.
- [Feature] **Nothing lost, nothing twice.** A bridge keeps every event in a queue on its own disk until this box has saved it. The box acknowledges an event only after the board's process has dealt with it, and ignores a number it has seen before. After an outage the backlog is played at the pace it happened, and the site is not made to watch every position of it go by.
- [Feature] **`ALNASL_NO_LOCAL_BOARDS=true`, for a server with no hall attached.** No serial ports, no loops, no simulators, no port scanning, and no row from older days started from a port. Boards come from bridges only; the tracker, the outbox, the destinations and the site run as anywhere else. Unset, a box behaves exactly as it did yesterday. With `ALNASL_UART=0` the serial library is not built, here or in Arkab.
- [Security] **A bridge's key is a hash here and never appears in a URL.** It travels in the first frame of the socket, where proxies do not write it down, and is compared in constant time. A join code is single use and dies after fifteen minutes whether or not anyone used it. The join link and the socket sit outside the PIN, because a bridge has no fingers; the code and the key are what keep them shut.
- [Feature] **Accounts, for a box that wants them.** Setup, *Accounts*, makes the first one: its holder becomes the box's owner and admin, and from that moment every page asks who is there - the kiosk screen on the box included, because "it came from localhost" stopped being an alibi. A box nobody gives an account stays exactly as open as it was, PIN and all; an update makes no accounts and closes no doors. Passwords are hashed with PBKDF2-SHA512 from OTP's own `:crypto`, so nothing new needs a C compiler. Sessions last thirty days, which outlives a weekend tournament and most phone batteries.
- [Feature] **Halls and roles.** The box's own ports are one hall, every bridge is in one, and so are its boards and tournaments. In each hall an account is an *owner* (bridges, invitations, everything), an *arbiter* (games, corrections, rounds, the OpenPairings and OpenResults links) or a *viewer* (watches, touches nothing). Admins see every hall and the box itself. A switcher at the top says which hall the pages are about, and the new *Halls* page shows each one at a glance: boards live, bridges connected, and which boards want an arbiter right now. Each hall has a tournament of its own for boards nobody has paired yet, and its own table numbers, so table 1 can exist twice without a fistfight.
- [Feature] **Invitations, by link.** An owner invites an address with a role; the box shows a link, good once and for a week, and sends no mail because it has no mail to send. Whoever opens it makes the account with a password or with 02cloud, or signs in and joins. A forgotten password is an admin's temporary one, shown once; the next sign-in asks for a real one.
- [Feature] **Sign in with 02cloud.** Keycloak, the same way OpenPairings does it, behind a button that appears only when `ALNASL_KEYCLOAK_CLIENT_SECRET` is set. It signs in the account with the same verified address and links the two. It never makes an account on its own say-so: knowing 02cloud is not the same as being invited.
- [Feature] **The server instance asks everybody.** With `ALNASL_NO_LOCAL_BOARDS=true` every page needs a sign-in, always. `ALNASL_ADMIN_EMAILS` names the admins, who may sign in with 02cloud before their account exists and get one on the spot; everyone else arrives by invitation, and an owner there can only invite addresses that already have an account. The same admin list works on a box, if you like having one.
- [Feature] **The phone's QR code signs the phone in.** With accounts, *Open this site on your phone* makes a sign-in code on request: good once, for five minutes, as you. A photo of the screen taken at minute six is a photo.
- [Change] **Every page sees one hall's worth.** Boards, rounds, games, the PGN page, Setup's tournaments and codes, the outbox's queue, the recordings, the labels and the log show the current hall's (an admin sees the box's own lines too). A tournament of another hall answers like one that does not exist, by address, by file download and by name in an event. A page left open while another hall's boards come and go stays about its own.
- [Change] **Adding, commanding and removing bridges is the hall owner's**, from that hall's page or Setup. An admin can move a bridge to another hall; its boards follow into that hall's own tournament and lose their table numbers, which belonged to the old hall.
- [Change] **A board is recognised by its serial within its hall, not across halls.** Carried from the box's own port to another hall's bridge, it gets a new row there and starts fresh; the first hall keeps its row, table and game, shown as not connected, until the board comes home. Between two bridges of one hall it is recognised and keeps its table, as before. Admins get no exception: whose table it is does not depend on who is asking. Duplicate serials and table numbers are only complained about within one hall.
- [Security] **The bridge door stays a bridge door.** `/join/<code>` and `/bridge/link` keep their own code-and-key credentials and are never behind the sign-in: a bridge has no fingers and no account. Every LiveView event is checked against the role it needs in the hall of every row it names before the page sees it, so a viewer cannot click their way into an arbiter's afternoon with a hand-made event.
- [Fix] Two test helpers carried default arguments nobody passed, and the compiler said so at every run. They take three arguments now, like everyone calling them.
- [Change] The older notes that pointed at `lib/alnasl/dgt/` and `Alnasl.Dgt.*` point at Arkab, where that code has lived since it moved.
- [Verified] **A real bridge, a real socket, a recorded game.** The same capture is read by the box's own replay driver and through an Arkab bridge in the same test: the line cut mid-game and the bridge restarted with half the game in its queue; this end's link and the board's own process killed mid-game; two servers on one bridge with one of them away. Every time, the game through the bridge is the game read here: the same moves, the same result, and the positions the board was given are the recording's, in order, with nothing missing and nothing applied twice.
- [Fix] **The hall picker is one menu again, not two.** An older dropdown rule for the connection actions shared the class of the picker's list, so the list floated off as a second bordered box, pinned to the right and spilling past the menu, with an empty divider on top. The older dropdown has its own class now, and the picker is a plain column under its button: halls, then *All halls*.

## [0.1.5] - 2026-10-15

- [Feature] **A *Details* button in the launcher window, on Windows and on the Mac.** It shows the program's own output (the end of `launcher.log`, or `logs/server.log` on a Mac, refreshed every second, left alone while you scroll up or select) over a few numbers in words: version, uptime, processor, memory, how long the database takes to answer, slow queries, pages open, tournaments, boards connected out of boards set up, board buses open, messages waiting for the results site, and the relay's state. *Copy* puts the numbers and the last 200 log lines on the clipboard; *Open log folder* shows the file. The Mac window follows the Mac's language, the Windows one follows Windows'. Counting the buses reads a registry and opens no serial port.
- [Feature] **`./alnasl.sh details` for a box without a window.** The same numbers and the last 20 log lines in a terminal, redrawn every two seconds until `q`; `--once` prints a snapshot, `--json` the raw answer. It reads `<data>/run/launcher.token`, so it runs as the user Alnasl runs as; `docs/launcher-details.md` has the line for an installed box.
- [Security] **The numbers have a door, and it is narrow.** `GET /_launcher/stats` answers a request from this machine only (the socket's address), only with a secret made at every start - handed over by the launcher, or written by the server to `<data>/run/launcher.token`, readable by its owner and removed on a clean stop - and only when no proxy header is on it, so a box behind a tunnel does not turn its public traffic into "this machine". The secret is compared in constant time. Everything else is a 404. The answer holds no token, no key, no player or game, and no address of the results server. The contract is `docs/launcher-details.md`.
- [Feature] **Toon, a cartoon theme, comes with every build.** A blue table under a faint grid, a cream sheet with a navy outline and a hard shadow, sticker panels, buttons that squash when pressed, a yellow pennant for the current page; Bangers for headings and Nunito for text, both under the SIL Open Font Licence and shipped with their licence file, so it works in a hall without internet. Tiles, boards, clocks and the move list get outlines and never move. It appears in the picker after the built-in themes (Tekenfilm in Dutch). It lives in `priv/theme_packs/toon`, a new place `Alnasl.ThemePacks` reads besides the user's themes folder, through the same scanner; a pack of your own with the folder name `toon` replaces it. `mix alnasl.theme_check --bundled` checks every bundled pack, and the tests do.

## [0.1.4] - 2026-10-15

- [Feature] **A Mac app.** Every push builds `Alnasl.app` for Apple Silicon (M1 and later) only, on GitHub's Macs, and a release attaches it as `alnasl-macos-aarch64.zip` (a fixed name, like the others; `SHA256SUMS` covers them). It behaves like the Windows program: a small window says Alnasl is running (in Dutch on a Dutch Mac), the browser opens once the site answers, a second double-click opens a tab instead of a second server, and *Stop Alnasl* asks first because the boards are not read while it is stopped. The serial library is in it. Nobody has opened it on a real Mac yet; `docs/macos.md` has the test list, the README and the Help page say how to install it (English and Dutch).
- [Feature] **The Mac keeps its data in `~/Library/Application Support/AlnaslData`**, and `Alnasl.DataHome` refuses any data folder inside a `.app`: an app is replaced whole by the next version and binned whole by an uninstall. The test reads the folder's name out of the Mac launcher too.
- [Change] **Not signed by Apple yet.** The first start needs "Open Anyway" in Privacy & Security; README, Help and `docs/macos.md` walk through it. Developer ID signing and notarisation are written into the release workflow and skip themselves until the Apple account's five secrets exist (`docs/releasing.md`).
- [Feature] **Updates on a Mac are by hand, and the notice says how.** Setup > Updates gives the size of the Mac zip and a Mac line: download, stop Alnasl, put the new app in place of the old one. Setup's "This copy of Alnasl" says *The Mac app*.
- [Change] **The Mac app lives in the Dock now.** It used to be a shell script that hid from the Dock on principle (`LSUIElement`), which made *Keep in Dock* a feature of every app on the Mac except this one. The app's executable is now a small native program (`rel/macos/Launcher.swift`, compiled by the build): a Dock icon with its running dot, a place in Cmd-Tab, *Options > Keep in Dock*, a window that closes without stopping anything and comes back when you click the icon, *Open in browser* on the icon's menu, and English or Dutch to match the Mac. *Stop Alnasl*, Cmd-Q and the Dock's *Quit* still ask first, since the boards are not read while it is stopped; logging out does not ask, because nobody is there to answer, and stops it cleanly. A force-quit takes the server along instead of leaving it reading boards where nobody can find it. The server's output moves to `logs/server.log` in the data folder, one run per file. Written without a Mac in the building, so the first compile is GitHub's and the first click is yours: `docs/macos.md` has the list.
- [Change] **The Mac icon is drawn, not converted.** The Dock got the Windows `.ico` pushed through `sips`: blurry, and edge to edge where every other icon keeps Apple's margin. `rel/windows/build_icon.py` now also draws `rel/macos/Alnasl-icon.png` from the mark, 1024 pixels with the tile at 824, and the app's icon is made from that.
- [Feature] **A Mac's serial ports are listed once.** macOS shows every adapter as `/dev/tty.X` and `/dev/cu.X`; the `tty.` one waits for a modem signal a board never sends, so only the `cu.` one is offered.
- [Feature] **Theme packs.** A theme can now be a folder instead of a release: drop one into `themes` in the data folder (or wherever `ALNASL_THEMES_DIR` says) and it is in the rail's picker and Setup's grid on the next page load, after the built-in five, with a strip of its own colours. It is OpenPairings' format with OpenPairings' scanner, which reads a stylesheet the way customs reads a suitcase: no `@import`, no URL but the pack's own fonts, every rule scoped to the pack's `[data-theme]`, and a refusal shown in the picker in grey with the reason, since nobody reads the log first. The stylesheet and fonts are served by the box itself and loaded only while the pack is the chosen theme; a pack that is removed sends the browsers that chose it back to Match device. `"dark": true` makes the browser's own controls dark as well. A `users` list in `theme.json` is ignored rather than refused: the box has no accounts, so a pack kept for somebody in OpenPairings is everybody's here. Nothing is installed by default. `docs/theme-packs.md` has the format and the token list; Help says where the folder is (English and Dutch).
- [Feature] **`mix alnasl.theme_check PATH`** tells a pack's author what the box will refuse, and then what nobody will be able to read: the whole palette defined, every text pairing the built-in themes are held to at 4.5:1, and the pack's own rules that set a colour on a background. The pairings moved out of the accessibility test into `AlnaslWeb.ThemeContrast`, so the test and the check cannot disagree about what readable means.

## [0.1.3] - 2026-10-15

- [Change] **The Dutch says "resultaat" and "versturen", like OpenPairings.** A game's result was an "uitslag" and sending was "verzenden"; both now use OpenPairings' words (resultaat, versturen, verstuurd), and the Verzendlijst page is called Wachtrij. The uitslagensite stays. Only Dutch changed; `docs/glossary-nl.md` records it.
- [Change] **The Dutch says "je" throughout.** Six strings (the loop warning, the Boards page, the results-page hint, the Outbox explanation, the bar above the page, the tournament text) still addressed the arbiter as "u" while the rest of the program said "je". They say "je" now; the one quote of Windows' own SmartScreen wording stays formal because Windows says it so. `docs/glossary-nl.md` records the decision and what changed in OpenPairings' Dutch to match.
- [Feature] **A team event reads like a team event.** OpenPairings has always sent each board's match and each player's team; the box threw both away and showed "Board 1 ... Board 32". The Round page now puts each match's boards under "Match 3: Team A - Team B", in the match's board order, with each player's team on the tile, the board's place in the match beside its number, and the match's score as the boards saw it finish - labelled as exactly that, since the score that counts is the one the arbiter types into OpenPairings. Nobody has run a real team event through it; it is built from the contract and tested against the fake.
- [Feature] **Tables that keep a match together.** Setup's tables of a team event have "Keep each match together" (on by default): the tables are counted match by match, so with four boards a match the fifth table is board 1 of match 2, whatever number the round gave that board. With OpenPairings numbering rounds the way it does, that is the same number as before; the box simply stops relying on it. Numbering by touching follows, and a new round finds each match on its tables again.
- [Feature] **"Ready for round N?" speaks of matches.** A missing board in a team event is "board 7 (match 2, board 3)", and a match that is only partly on electronic boards is said, not refused.
- [Feature] **Team tags in the PGN.** A team game's PGN - the box's own, the one OpenPairings keeps beside a report, the one Lichess gets - carries `WhiteTeam` and `BlackTeam`, and `Round` as round and board (`3.6`), the way OpenPairings and OpenResults write `Round` for a game with moves. What the results site and OpenPairings are sent is otherwise unchanged: the site keys a live game by round and the board's number through the round, OpenPairings by game id, and neither needs a match to be told.
- [Fix] **One tournament reached through two codes is one tournament.** A group's code and the tournament's own code, or a desktop OpenPairings' code for this computer and its code for the hall's network, made two rows of one tournament, and every game was reported twice. The second code now makes no row and says so in words - "This code reaches Open, which this box already has through ..." - in Setup and in the log. The same tournament is the same address and id, or the same id with the same version, or a game id in common. "Use this code for Open" moves the tournament to the new code with its boards and games (a code withdrawn and made again), and the old code goes when it reaches nothing else. A tournament that joins a group whose box already has its own code is caught the same way, and a tournament the old code stops listing moves to the code that still lists it instead of going grey.
- [Fix] **A box that already has a tournament twice has it once after the update.** At start-up, before the first poll, the later row is merged into the first: boards, games and what waits in the outbox move; a game whose place the first row already filled with a game that began stays on the box as the next game there, never replacing it; an empty game in a taken place is dropped. The log says what was merged and how much moved.
- [Change] **The Boards page fits a phone.** The table was 1,100 pixels wide and slid sideways inside its frame on a 375-pixel screen, which is not how one walks a hall. Below 860 pixels every board is a card instead: its table number large and typeable at the top, the name beside it, then state (shape and word), the board of the round, the reading, the signal, the start position and the buttons, each button 44 pixels tall and Remove on a line of its own. Wider than that nothing changed: the same table, the same ids. "Back" and "Sound" on the two touch pages are now 44 pixels tall on a phone as well.
- [Change] **The Dutch says what OpenPairings says.** Where both programs name the same thing, Alnasl's Dutch now uses OpenPairings' word: het kastje (was "de box"), uitslagensite (was also "uitslagenserver"), relaissleutel, paring and paringsprogramma (were "indeling" and "indelingsprogramma"; a round is "gepaard", not "ingedeeld"), afgeleid and geraden for the moves it worked out and the ones it had to guess (were "gereconstrueerd" and "gegokt"), Help (was "Hulp"), Klaar (was "Gereed" on the state and "Klaar" in the Help line that described it), Wacht and Sluiten. `docs/glossary-nl.md` has the table, what was left different on purpose and what OpenPairings might change in return. Only Dutch changed; the English texts did not.

## [0.1.1] - 2026-10-12

- [Fix] **The box no longer invents moves for pieces that did not move.** On a board that stayed connected, a second black move in a row was explained by sending White's bishop to a6 and back, marked as a guess. A reconstructed line may now only move pieces the readings saw leave their squares; a piece going out and straight back is not a line at all, anywhere; and moves without that evidence are considered only when the board really was gone (disconnected, or three polls missed in a row on a loop), and are then red.
- [Feature] **An alarm that says what the board shows.** "Black moved (Nc6), but it is White's turn" and "A move that is not legal: white bishop c1-g5" (with why: something in the way, not how that piece moves, its own piece there, its king left in check) - on the board's page, the Round tile and in the log, after two seconds instead of eight of "does not match". Nothing is added to the game, and it is gone when the piece is back. "A move is missing before this" goes to where moves are typed.
- [Change] **"Gap" on a move means the board was gone**, not that three moves were found at once or that somebody thought for twenty minutes. Moves found across a disconnection are red, whatever else is true of them.
- [Change] **"The board is right" and "Check them" offer no line the readings do not support** either.
- [Feature] **After a connect code the box says which boards play in the tournament, or asks.** The first real connection left the one board in the box's own tournament, reading a game that went nowhere, with nothing saying so. Now: one tournament and an unused own tournament, and every board plays there at once (its table number is its board; a board without a number gets the next free one), said in a line under the bar with an Undo; a group of tournaments or a game under way gets "Which tables play in ...?" on Setup with ranges filled in, and a tournament can be declared to have no boards here. - [Feature] **A board that plays in no linked tournament is never silent.** The bar on every page, the Boards page, the Round tiles and "Ready for round N?" say "Board 1 is not playing in ...", with a button to put it there. - [Change] **The statuses are about the tournament in use.** The bar names OpenPairings (connected, no answer, code refused) and the results site (sending, waiting, offline, "no relay key yet") instead of one "Not set up" that read as the connection just made having failed; the unused own tournament is no longer mentioned. The Outbox has a "Linked tournaments" panel, and Setup shows a missing relay key as a warning with the action spelled out (OpenPairings, Settings, Live boards, The results site); it clears by itself at the next poll once a key arrives, a rotated key replaces the stored one, and the games recorded meanwhile are queued in full.
- [Fix] **"Send now" always answers, and says why when it cannot.** It used to flash "Trying now" whatever the state, so on a box with nothing to send it looked broken. The Outbox says in one line that sending is automatic. OpenPairings and the results site were on by default already, and an explicit off is kept.
- [Feature] **"Live · N ms" in the bar**: the browser's connection to the box, measured every few seconds, and "Reconnecting..." when it drops.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-12

The first release. This is everything so far, in the order it
was built; the commit messages have the long version.

- [Feature] **An installer, a portable copy and an updater.** Windows: `Alnasl-Setup.exe` (per user, no administrator, a Start menu entry and a small window that is the stop button) and a zip that installs nothing. Linux, x86_64 and arm64: one line that installs a service under `/opt/alnasl`, as its own user, in the group that may open serial ports. The program asks GitHub every half hour whether a newer version is out and says so; it downloads nothing until you say yes, tells you the size first, and refuses to restart under a game in progress unless you insist. The question can be switched off (Setup, Updates). The data is in a folder of its own that no installer, update or uninstall touches. Nothing is signed: Windows will say so, once.
- [Feature] **The switch for the hall's network is on the Setup page.** "Let other machines in the hall open this site" used to be an environment variable set before the program started, which is not something an installed program has. It needs a PIN first, and takes effect at the next start. `ALNASL_LISTEN`, where it is set, still decides.
- [Feature] **A box serves several tournaments, and OpenPairings tells it who plays.** A connect code pasted once; pairings, colours and the rate of play arrive by themselves, a round starts when it is paired, and what each board saw goes back - moves, clocks, PGN and the result on the board, which is shown there and never entered.
- [Feature] **More than one place for the games to go.** The results server, OpenPairings and a Lichess broadcast, each with its own queue, its own switch and its own line saying whether it is connected and why not.
- [Feature] **A board that runs ahead is caught up, not stopped.** Moves that went by unseen are looked for and marked - reconstructed when one line fits, guessed when several did - and a tap on a guessed move asks which it was. A move read on the square beside the right one is rewritten. A piece that leaves the board for a while is not an alarm.
- [Feature] **Which board is which.** A board is known by its serial number and keeps its table number across ports and restarts; numbering a hall by touching the boards in order, from a phone; "Ready for round N?" with the reasons when it is not.
- [Feature] **Ending a game and beginning the next** on the same board, by the arbiter or by setting the pieces up again; fast play, with a preset for games of a few minutes.
- [Feature] **The management site**: Round, a board's page with the repair tools, Boards, Outbox, PGN, Recordings, Log, Setup, Help; English and Dutch; five colour themes; a PIN for other machines.
- [Feature] **Several boards on one cable: a port is added, and that is all.** Boards, Add a board, choose the port: the box asks the port who is on it and gives every board that answers a row of its own - one board is read as one board, two or more as a loop. No bus address is asked for anywhere. A board plugged into the chain later gets its row within ten seconds; one taken off keeps its row, which says why it is silent. An older board is known by its serial number on a loop as anywhere; a newer one (firmware 4) by the number it answers to, until it has been plugged in alone once.
- [Fix] **A loop added as one board is no longer a board with a bad cable.** Every board on a loop answers a single board's questions at once, and what arrived was nonsense that now and then looked like a position. The box asks who is on the line before anything else, reads nothing when it is more than one, and says so: "More than one board answers on this port: it is a loop", with "Read it as a loop" beside it.
- [Change] **`mix alnasl.dgt --bus` is gone; `mix alnasl.loop` is the loop's tool** (`--discover`, `--watch`, `--leave`, `--probe`). It sends questions and mode switches and nothing that writes to a board or a clock.
- [Verified] **A loop of two real boards has been read** (2026-10-12: 06310 with firmware 1.9 and a newer board with firmware 4.0, on one serial line): the frames, finding the boards with no prior knowledge, who they are, what stands on each, 16 ms a question and none unanswered in three minutes through the site. Nobody was at the boards and no clock was attached: a move as it is played, a clock and more than two boards on a loop have not been seen, and the box asks for the whole board every few seconds as well, so that a move there can be late but not wrong. `docs/real-loop-2026-10-12.md` has every byte - and the one thing that went wrong, a question that made the newer board restart itself.
- [Verified] **The serial driver has read one real board and clock** (2026-10-12): handshake, position, piece movements, clock. A whole game on real hardware, and a loop of several boards on one cable, have not been played yet.
