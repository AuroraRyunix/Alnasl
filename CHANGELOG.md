# Changelog

What changed in Alnasl, newest first. The format loosely follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and each entry is
tagged so that a version can be skimmed:

| tag | meaning |
|---|---|
| [Feature] | something new you can do |
| [Fix] | something that was broken |
| [Change] | existing behaviour works differently |
| [Removed] | something is gone |
| [Security] | a hole closed, or judged not to be one |
| [Verified] | checked against the real thing, no code change |

A version is released when its number is in `mix.exs` **and** it has a
section here (`## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-03`): the release's page on GitHub is that
section, word for word. See [docs/releasing.md](docs/releasing.md).

## [Unreleased]

- [Fix] **A piece set down between two squares is less often blamed on the wrong square.** When the board reads a piece one square off, the tracker has always been able to put the move right once a later move made no sense. Two cases it then got wrong. A bishop read on d3 and put right as c4 kept that first guess even after somebody straightened it onto e2, in plain view; the move is now asked about again around where it was read, which is what the board actually said. And a rook nudged home from h6 to h5 on its own side's time reads as a legal move, so when it then really went to h6 the board looked exactly like a takeback - and the box believed the takeback, a player's whole move down. A move taken back is never pressed for; a move made is. With a clock that pressed after the "move", it is now read as what it was: a straightening, and a move. Without a clock it is a takeback, as before, because nothing says otherwise. In the fault matrix the misread suite loses three wrong games of nine, wrongly marked moves fall from 45 to 7 and alarms from 39 to 20; without a clock, where only the first of the two can help, it loses one; every other row is the same to the game. docs/design.md has the table.
- [Fix] **The Mac app no longer gives way to an older server in silence.** Replace the app with a newer one while the old server is still running and the new one found the port answering, concluded that all was well, opened the browser, and left. The browser then showed the old version, and the new app had politely vanished. The server now says its version in a header (to this machine only), and the app compares it with its own: the same version is a genuine second start and behaves as before; any other, or none, gets a window saying which Alnasl is still running, with *Stop it and start this one* and *Open it anyway*. Stopping works on a server the app did not start, finds it by the port, refuses anything whose command line is not Alnasl's, gives it 25 seconds to leave cleanly, and only then insists. Untested on a real Mac until someone double-clicks it.
- [Change] **"Fair-play screening" is now just "Fair play".** In the menu, the page titles, the buttons and the manual, in English and in Dutch. The screening was always more of a number than a verdict, and the longer name promised a rigour the shorter one does not pretend to. Routes, settings and data keep their names.

## [0.5.1] - 2026-10-20

- [Change] **Fair-play screening has its own place in the menu.** It used to hide behind a button on a hall's page, which is where one looks for a hall and not for a suspicion. It is now an item under Manage, for owners of at least one hall and nobody else (an admin who owns no hall still sees nothing, as before), and opens on a list of the halls you own: on or off, engine or no engine, and a link to each tournament's report. The button on the hall page is gone; nothing points at the new place from the old one, because the menu does that.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-10-20

- [Feature] **Fair-play screening: a number, some counts, and a stern word that it proves nothing.** A hall's owner can switch it on (off until then, hall by hall); Alnasl then analyses each move with Stockfish 19 as soon as the tracker is sure of it, in the background, at the lowest priority the computer offers, and never once in the tracker's way. The report lists the players of a tournament, through a round or for that round alone: counted moves, how often the engine's first choice was played against what the rating predicts, average loss against what was expected, a score with its interval, and how many moves were left out and why (opening, repeated, forced, decided, under a minute, guessed or rewritten by the tracker, no rating). "Worth a closer look" from 2, "statistically very unusual" above 5, with the positions behind it on boards. Until there is a calibration for the engine the report shows the counts and says plainly that there is no score. Every page says that this is not the method FIDE has approved (Art. 1.6), and the manual has a chapter on what to do with an unusual number, which is mostly: nothing, to anybody, in the hall.
- [Security] **Fair play belongs to the hall's owner, and not to the admin.** Not arbiters, not viewers, and not the hosted server's admin in a hall that is not theirs, though the admin is "owner" everywhere else. The page is in nobody else's menu, answers anybody else as a page that is not there, and the data are not in the public pages, the PGN files, what goes to OpenResults or Lichess, the log or the outbox. Every opening of a report is written down with who and when, and the report shows the latest.
- [Feature] **The engine is downloaded, not shipped.** "Download Stockfish 19" fetches the official release, checks its SHA256 against the one published and its `bench` against the build the numbers were calibrated for. No internet in the hall: "Choose a file" takes the same archive from a USB stick and makes the same checks. Stockfish is credited on the page, in NOTICE and in the manual.
- [Feature] **Fair-play data have a shelf life.** The analysis and the audit of a tournament are deleted thirty days after its last round (`ALNASL_FAIR_PLAY_RETENTION_DAYS`), once a day. An owner with a case going to FIDE's Fair Play Commission can keep a tournament up to 365 days after its last round, with a reason that is kept with it. `ALNASL_FAIR_PLAY_ENGINES` sets how many engines run at once (cores less one on a box, one on the server).

## [0.4.5] - 2026-10-20

- [Fix] **Stopping from Ascella, an update or a logout no longer hangs on "Stopping".** On a Mac, the window waited for its server to end inside the very queue that was to bring the news, so the server ended and the window kept saying "Stopping" until somebody force-quit it. Quit from the menu never had the problem, which is how it survived this long. Any stop that hangs anyway now ends after forty seconds.

## [0.4.4] - 2026-10-19

- [Change] **Arkab 0.3.4 inside.** Nothing a hall notices: the library's new parts are for the bridge, which now says "in another hall" instead of shrugging.
- [Feature] **Intel Macs, from macOS 10.13.** `alnasl-macos-x86_64.zip` joins the Apple Silicon one on every release. The Intel runner takes thirteen minutes where the others take a few, so it builds only when a release goes out or somebody starts the workflow by hand, not on every push. The update notice picks the zip by the Mac's architecture (an Intel build under Rosetta counts as Apple Silicon), and no longer tells Intel Macs there is nothing for them. Untested on a real Mac that old, which docs/macos.md says in plain words.

## [0.4.3] - 2026-10-19

- [Change] **Arkab 0.3.3 inside.** A loop board that misses a question now says in the log what came back instead, so a mute board and a mangled answer can be told apart.

- [Change] **A loop is read as a loop without asking.** When two boards answered on a port added as one board, the page explained the problem, refused to read anything and offered a button for the only possible answer. It now just does it; the button stays for the rare port that will not cooperate.
- [Fix] **A board through a bridge shows its signal as a percentage again.** 0.4.2 decided "port of its own" by a field only local loops carry, so every bridged loop board was told it had "never dropped", which was true and useless.

## [0.4.2] - 2026-10-19

- [Fix] **Signal for a board that reaches the box through a bridge counts what it answered.** A bridge sends one "answered" a second but every miss, so a loop board polled sixty times a second showed 95% with nine misses, and one with a handful of misses showed 17%. The bridge's answer now says how many answers it stands for, and Signal adds that many. (Needs a bridge that says so, which is the next Arkab; an older one counts as before.)
- [Change] **A board on a port of its own shows "dropped N times" instead of a percentage.** It is asked for its whole position every three seconds and counts a miss only when the cable goes, so 97% meant nothing in particular. The percentage stays for boards on a loop, where it does.
- [Change] **The live boards are one click away.** The bar's link to the results site went to the tournament's front page, a click short of where everyone actually goes during a round, behind an arrow nobody recognised as a link. It now says "Live boards" and goes straight there, and each tournament's card on Setup has the same link beside the results page.

## [0.4.1] - 2026-10-19

- [Fix] **A stop no longer waits fifteen seconds for every open page to hang up.** Bandit's default courtesy to open connections, which on a board relay means every spectator, made the Mac app's "Stopping..." look like a hang and every restart slower than it needed to be. Open pages now get three seconds, and the Mac launcher writes into the log how long a stop took, or that it had to insist.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-10-19

- [Security] **A role that changed now counts.** The rights of an open page were worked out once, when it opened, so an arbiter who was demoted or taken out of a hall kept entering results there until they reloaded, which on a tournament day can be a long time. Any change of someone's membership, and the deletion of a hall, now sends their open pages away; the browser reconnects by itself and gets what they may do now.
- [Security] **"Send again, whatever is held" keeps to your hall.** It used to re-send every game that OpenPairings had called stale, in every hall on the box, which let an arbiter of one hall overwrite a correction made in another's tournament. It now covers the tournaments of the hall you are in; an admin, and a box without accounts, still get all of them.
- [Security] **The PIN is no longer a guessing game.** After five wrong tries an address has to wait, five seconds and then twice as long each time, a quarter of an hour at most, even if the next try is right; the box as a whole takes at most forty wrong tries a minute. New PINs need six characters. An older, shorter one keeps working, and Setup, Access asks you to replace it.
- [Security] **A bridge stays in its hall.** The code of another hall used to take over a bridge that was still in a hall, as long as it said which bridge it was, and that name is no secret. The server now refuses (403) until the bridge has been removed from its hall, as an admin can move it.
- [Security] **A stranger's listing is nobody's twin.** A second server could claim a tournament of another hall by listing the same id and version, and then take it over with "Use this code". Two codes on different servers are one tournament now only when both are in the same hall, and using a code for a tournament of another hall needs a role there.
- [Security] **Captures of a removed board** can be deleted, replayed or exported by an admin only, as they can be listed and downloaded by an admin only.
- [Security] **The declared admin's address is for an admin to invite**, everywhere. An owner of a hall could invite that address on a box, open the link, pick a password and be admin. Invitations of that kind made earlier no longer make the account either.
- [Security] **The hosted server no longer knocks on its own doors.** An address typed for OpenPairings or a results page that resolves to loopback, a private range or link-local (also through a redirect) is refused on the server, with a message that says so. A box keeps allowing any address, because a desktop OpenPairings lives at 127.0.0.1.
- [Change] **The OpenPairings address offered under "Connect an account" is remembered per hall, and only after an account really connected.** It used to be one for the whole box, set the moment anybody typed something. The address you used before has to be typed once more.

## [0.3.2] - 2026-10-18

- [Fix] **Stopping no longer ends in a red stack trace.** On the way out the launcher-details process tidied up its secret by asking the application controller, which at that point is busy dying and answers everyone with an exit. The secret goes with the VM anyway, so now it lets the question go unanswered and leaves quietly.

- [Change] **New address.** The repository moved to github.com/teapot-chess; old links still redirect, out of politeness rather than obligation.
## [0.3.1] - 2026-10-18

- [Fix] **Quitting Alnasl on the Mac stops Alnasl, and whatever it started.** The launcher script signalled the server and nobody else, so a helper program could outlive the thing that asked for it; it now reads the whole tree under the server before saying goodbye, and after twenty seconds (or a clean exit, which is no proof of anything) SIGKILLs all of it. The app's own last resort, ten seconds later, did the same trick with only the script, and left the server to launchd; it now takes the tree as well.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-10-18

- [Feature] **A Setup wizard, first in Setup.** It asks what the hall looks like - one hall and one tournament, several tournaments, several halls or rooms - how many Arkab bridges, roughly how many boards, and what else is wanted (results for spectators, Lichess, a Polis print station, a Nunki announcer, phones behind a PIN on a box). Then it lists the steps for exactly that hall, in order, "Bridge 2 of 3" included, each with one button to where it is done. Every tick comes from what is really there: a bridge that has connected, the boards that arrived, the tournaments a connect code brought, table numbers on every board, a results page with its relay key. The two Alnasl cannot see, the print station and the announcer, are ticked by hand and say so, rather than pretend. The answers are kept per hall, so it can be reopened, re-answered and still tick. A step your role cannot do keeps its tick and tells you who can.
- [Change] **Setup opens on the wizard until the hall is set up**, and an address that leads nowhere leads there too. Once every step is done it opens on Tournaments again, as before; the wizard stays in the list for the day something changes.
- [Change] **The word "bridge" now comes with its name.** This server's Edition line says the boards come from Arkab bridges and links to the wizard; "Add a bridge" lists what to do on the other side (install Arkab beside the boards, open its page on port 4050, paste the link under "Join a server" or scan the code), with the download; the server's empty Boards page links to the wizard. Nobody new should have to guess that a bridge is a program you install.
- [Change] The manual, in English and Dutch, describes the wizard and what ticks each step, and no longer says Arkab has no download: it has had releases since 0.2.0.

## [0.2.2] - 2026-10-17

- [Fix] **The Mac app opens on macOS 11 and newer**, not only on 15. The Erlang inside is compiled for 11 now, SQLite too, and the build fails, naming the file, if anything in the app asks for a newer macOS. The ready-made runtime had demanded the latest macOS all along; it just never said so to anyone who had it.
- [Change] The docs and the screens now call them what they are to the person using them: live boards.
- [Change] **One word per thing.** The program is *Alnasl* in every sentence the server can show too, so the server no longer apologises for being a box: "Arbiter in Alnasl: move 12 changed", "This game stays in Alnasl", "This page lost its connection to Alnasl". OpenResults is the *results site* everywhere, including the Test connection steps and the Outbox's errors, which used to call it "the server" - a word that now means the hosted Alnasl and nothing else. The tournament a hall keeps for unpaired boards is *the hall's own tournament*, not "This box". The page in the rail called Results is called Results at the top as well. The Dutch follows ("Alnasl" for "het kastje" in shared sentences, *Resultaten* for the page, the last "box" and "indeling" gone). The list lives in `docs/glossary.md`, and a test reads both catalogues for the retired words, because a glossary nobody checks is a suggestion.
- [Change] The manual, in both languages, says what the screens say now: Alnasl where it said the box for things the server does too, the Results and Games pages by those names, the hall's own tournament, and a short "Words used here" at the end of Getting started. The Dutch manual lost its last *indelingen*. The README's page table caught up as well.
- [Change] **An empty list says what it means and what to do next.** Setup's tournaments (none yet, none here, none matching the search) and connections, a hall's bridges, people and invitations, the Halls page for an account in no hall, the Log, the Results page's waiting and sent lists, the round's games on Games, a search of the recordings that finds nothing and the label sheet all get a title and a sentence in the style the Live and Boards pages already had, with a button where there is one obvious next step. A blank panel was technically accurate and of no use to anyone.
- [Change] The PIN page and the page a bridge's join link opens in a browser wear the same door frame as signing in, and can show a flash.
- [Change] The templates' inline styles are classes in the stylesheet, with spacing tokens; the classes that were used but never styled have CSS, and the progress bar along the top takes the theme's accent instead of a teal nobody chose. Nothing moves, give or take a tenth of a rem.

## [0.2.1] - 2026-10-17

- [Fix] **The account menu is drawn over the page, not under it.** The rail is sticky, which makes it a stacking context, and without a level of its own it was painted in document order: the manual's contents, sticky and later in the page, landed on top of the theme buttons. The rail now has a level above the page, so what opens from it, the account menu and the hall picker, stays above everything on every page and theme.
- [Change] **An empty Boards page on the server talks about bridges.** It used to invite you to plug an electronic board into a serial port the server does not have. It now says boards arrive through bridges, lists this hall's bridges with their state and how many boards each reports, and links to the hall's Bridges tab; a hall with none is told so in one sentence. The box keeps its serial ports.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-17

- [Security] **Sharing a tournament is not sharing the keys to the building.** The best role in any hall a tournament is played in still counts for its share in that hall, and for nothing else: giving the current hall's tables away, adding a code or touching its bridges needs a role in the hall you are standing in; removing the tournament, its Lichess key, starting it by itself and the next round need one in its home hall; a result or a new game for board N needs one where board N is played. An arbiter of the annex who is a viewer in the main hall could seat the annex's tournament on the main hall's tables with one hand-made event; the event now gets the same answer the button would. *Also play it in this hall* works for the arbiter it is shown to, who until now was offered a button and refused by it - the hall's own code reaching the tournament is the permission. *Add a hall* starts after the tables where another tournament's boards sit, not only after the ranges it bothered to write down, and refuses in a sentence to take a table whose board has a game under way. A merge that moves a hall to later boards renumbers that hall's boards at once, a hall whose own tournament is played next door cannot be deleted from under it, rolling the placements migration back keeps the ranges of tournaments from before halls, a server no longer asks GitHub for updates it cannot install, and Live asks the database a handful of questions a refresh instead of a few per board.
- [Change] **A frame that knows which hall it is in, and a language that belongs to whoever reads it.** The rail is grouped - Play (Live, Boards, Games), Send (Results), Manage (Halls, Setup), Look back (Log, Recordings), Help - and its top names the box, or says *server* on the server. With more than one hall, a hall picker shows the hall on show and how many of its boards are live, lists your halls with your role and their live boards (a search field from seven on), and switching keeps you on the page you were on instead of dumping you on the front page to start over. The account button holds who you are, your role here, Account, the language, the colour theme and Sign out; the theme picker in Setup and the EN/NL at the foot of the rail are gone, because three ways to do one thing is two ways to wonder which one won. The language is per person now: kept with the account when there is one, in a browser cookie when there is not, set by a form instead of a link a crawler could follow. Whoever chose nothing reads the box's default, which an admin still sets in Setup; one arbiter switching to Dutch no longer switches the whole hall with them. On a phone the rail becomes a top bar and a tab bar - Live, Boards, Results, More - and nothing is out of reach. Notices stack, two at a time, the rest folded behind *N more notices*, and a notice about tournaments names three and sends you to Setup for the rest. Live shows the tournaments placed in this hall and, of those, the boards here and the games played here, so a tournament split over two rooms no longer shows each room the other's half. It offers a list next to the tiles - table, players, last move, clocks, state - remembered per browser and chosen for you from 24 boards on; the QR code waits behind *Share* instead of being drawn for every tournament on every refresh; and a move redraws its own tile, not the hall.
- [Change] **Two editions, one answer, and a front page that stops listing the universe.** `Alnasl.edition/0` says `:box` or `:server`, Setup's *This copy of Alnasl* says it too, and the server stops offering what needs a serial port or an installer: *Add a board*, the serial ports, simulators (on Boards and on PGN), Updates and starting with Windows, the PIN and the hall network, and the box's name, which is a fixed name there and turns the panel into *This server*. Absent, not greyed, and the events refuse when a clever client sends them anyway. The Live page draws a tile only for a board that exists here or has a game; pairings with neither become one line ("26 pairings without a board here"), and a tournament with neither gets no section and no tab, only a line saying how many there are. A box with one tournament still draws a tile for every pairing, and a tournament opened by its own address is always shown, counted like the rest. Ten tournaments of forty pairings used to be four hundred tiles for four boards.
- [Change] **Setup stops being a junk drawer.** Twelve panels on one page are seven sections, each with its own address and a list beside it (a drop-down on a phone): `/setup/tournaments`, `connections`, `results`, `this-box`, `access`, `updates`, `advanced`. A section the edition or the role cannot use is not drawn: the server has no Access and no Updates, and only an admin sees Updates and Advanced. Tournaments are a line each - name, a dot for running, round, boards, a tables chip - and open for the details, the halls and tables, and the automatic start; the ones with boards on this box come first, the rest are one folded line, the finished and the not yet started sit behind a toggle, and from the ninth tournament there is a search field. Connections are a line each with a state and a menu (Test, Retry, Remove), and *+ Add* holds the two ways in: a pasted code, or an OpenPairings account. Gone from Setup: the colour theme (it lives in the rail), and the bridges, who were only ever the hall page's guests; a button to the hall page is left in their place. Everything else is where it was, one click further, which is the usual price of tidying.
- [Change] **Every page asks which hall it is in.** Boards, the numbering and finding pages (which used to read every board on the box and number whichever one felt a piece lift), PGN, the Outbox with its counts and the number in the frame, the Log and the recordings now answer for the hall you are looking at, and the Log and the recordings take a tick, *All my halls*, for the rest. They also have an end: fifty lines or files a page and a search box, instead of the newest 300 that were filtered for your halls afterwards. A board's tournament select lists what is played in the hall and the tournament the board is in. *Halls* is a table instead of cards, and a hall has tabs - People, Invitations, Bridges, Settings - each with an address of its own. A box without accounts sees everything, as before.
- [Security] **The Games page asks where the board is, too.** Attaching a game to board N, or playing it on a simulator as board N, now needs your role in the hall board N is played in, the rule the Live page learned a while ago; an arbiter of the annex who watches the main hall no longer writes over the main hall's board 3 from a pasted PGN. One game on a simulator also plays for the tournament on show, as *Play them all* always did, instead of wandering off to the box's own. The count beside Results in the rail is the badge's count, from one place, so the two can no longer argue about what is waiting; and the links into Setup land on a section that exists, the bridges button on the hall's Bridges tab.
- [Feature] **A hall can be deleted.** On its page, an owner or an admin types the hall's name, the button wakes up, and the people, invitations, join codes and boards of the hall go in one transaction. A hall with bridges, tournaments or an OpenPairings connection left refuses and says which ("2 bridges and 1 tournament"), so the button cannot throw a game away, and the box's own hall cannot be deleted at all. Whoever was standing in it is moved to another hall of theirs, and open pages notice. Nothing is lost that was not asked for twice.
- [Feature] **One tournament in several halls, several tournaments in one hall.** A tournament no longer lives in one hall on one run of tables; Setup, under each tournament, *Halls and tables* lists where it is played: a hall, its tables, the first board they play. Boards 1-8 in the main hall and 9-16 in the annex are two rows of one tournament, and table 4 of the annex plays board 12. *Add a hall* offers only halls where you arbitrate or own, and starts at the next free tables and the board after the last one placed. Two tournaments on one table, one board in two halls, and a tournament with no hall left are refused; the Open on 1-8 and the under-twelves on 9-16 work exactly as before, because that was never the hard part. A tournament shows up in every hall it is played in, your role for it is the best one you have in any of them, and a board is still recognised only in its own hall - sharing a tournament is not sharing a room. A second hall's code for a tournament the box already has offers *Also play it in this hall* instead of merely pointing out that it exists elsewhere. Existing boxes are upgraded to one row per tournament, seated exactly as they were.
- [Feature] **A manual, with chapters, in English and Dutch.** Help is no longer one long page that was a guide before it was a manual. It is seven chapters - getting started, boards and ports, bridges, following games, the links to OpenPairings and OpenResults, accounts and halls, recording and troubleshooting - in the look of OpenPairings' manual, with a contents page, a search (press `/`), an outline per chapter, all in whichever of the two languages the box speaks. Beside the title of every other page sits a small *?* that opens the manual at the section about it, so nobody has to remember which chapter the Outbox lives in. Written from what the code does, which turned out to be a more modest feature list than the ambition.
- [Feature] **Connect a whole OpenPairings account instead of copying codes one by one.** Setup, *Connections*, *+ Add*, *Connect an OpenPairings account*: type the address once (or set `ALNASL_OPENPAIRINGS_URL`; the last one used is offered after that), sign in over there, press Allow, and you are back with every current tournament that account may edit - including the ones it has not made yet. The list shows it as "All your tournaments on host (N)". The answer that comes back is good once, for two minutes, and only for the browser that asked; a stale, stolen or cancelled one adds nothing and says so in a sentence. Owners and arbiters of the hall only, checked when you leave and again when you return, because people do change their minds in between.
- [Change] **The board drivers moved out, to Arkab.** The board protocol, the single board, the loop, the emulator, the transports, the capture format and the replay player now live in Arkab, a library of their own, so the code that reads a board exists once - here, and on a bridge. A board on the box's own port reads byte for byte as it did; only the module names changed (`Alnasl.Dgt.*` is `Arkab.Dgt.*`, and so on). The capture files are the same files.
- [Feature] **Bridges: boards from somewhere else.** A hall's page (or Setup, for the hall you are looking at), *Bridges*, *Add a bridge* shows a link and its QR code, good for fifteen minutes and one bridge. Paste or scan it on an Arkab bridge - a Raspberry Pi beside the boards of another hall - and that is the whole setup: the bridge fetches its own key, dials out over one WebSocket, and its boards turn up as rows of their own, numbered and linked like any other. A board carried from the box's own port to a bridge is recognised by its serial and keeps its table and its game. One box takes any number of bridges, and a bridge may belong to more than one box.
- [Feature] **Commands down the same line.** From the Bridges panel: read a port, read it again, forget it, and send every board again in full. Answers come back in words, including "no".
- [Feature] **Remove, from either side, ends it for both.** Removed here, a connected bridge is told to forget this box, and one that dials in later is told the same thing. Removed on the bridge, it is gone here too, with its boards. Nobody is left politely knocking on a door that has been bricked up.
- [Feature] **Nothing lost, nothing twice.** A bridge keeps every event in a queue on its own disk until this box has saved it. The box acknowledges an event only after the board's process has dealt with it, and ignores a number it has seen before. After an outage the backlog is played at the pace it happened, and the site is not made to watch every position of it go by.
- [Feature] **`ALNASL_NO_LOCAL_BOARDS=true`, for a server with no hall attached.** No serial ports, no loops, no simulators, no port scanning, and no row from older days started from a port. Boards come from bridges only; the tracker, the outbox, the destinations and the site run as anywhere else. Unset, a box behaves exactly as it did yesterday. With `ALNASL_UART=0` the serial library is not built, here or in Arkab.
- [Security] **A bridge's key is a hash here and never appears in a URL.** It travels in the first frame of the socket, where proxies do not write it down, and is compared in constant time. A join code is single use and dies after fifteen minutes whether or not anyone used it. The join link and the socket sit outside the PIN, because a bridge has no fingers; the code and the key are what keep them shut.
- [Feature] **Accounts, for a box that wants them.** Setup, *Accounts*, makes the first one: its holder becomes the box's owner and admin, and from that moment every page asks who is there - the kiosk screen on the box included, because "it came from localhost" stopped being an alibi.
